# Benchmark: with and without the skill, 15 October 2026

Each eval in `evals.json` was run three times with the skill and three times without it: 24 runs in all. Every run was graded strictly against that eval's assertions.

## Results

| Eval | Assertions | With skill, per run | With skill, mean ± sd | Baseline, per run | Baseline, mean ± sd | Difference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Act on a third-party audit | 26 | 26, 25, 25 | 97.4% ± 2.2 | 18, 19, 18 | 70.5% ± 2.2 | +26.9 pts |
| 2. Plan a new microsite | 24 | 23, 22, 24 | 95.8% ± 4.2 | 5, 5, 6 | 22.2% ± 2.4 | +73.6 pts |
| 3. CMS-editable banner | 18 | 16, 18, 14 | 88.9% ± 11.1 | 13, 10, 10 | 61.1% ± 9.6 | +27.8 pts |
| 4. Can these checks be trusted? | 19 | 19, 16, 19 | 94.7% ± 9.1 | 13, 13, 13 | 68.4% ± 0.0 | +26.3 pts |
| All, pooled | 87 | 247 of 261 | 94.6% | 143 of 261 | 54.8% | +39.8 pts |

The sd is the sample standard deviation of the three runs' pass rates. With three runs per condition it is a rough guide, not a confidence interval. In every eval the lowest with-skill run scored higher than the highest baseline run.

The earlier assertions, graded leniently from one run per condition, gave 31 of 31 with the skill and about 26 of 31 without it.

## What separates the conditions

Of the 87 assertions:

- 41 passed in all six runs. They check that the answer gets the basics right (logo alt, JSON-LD present, dead links, the em-dash rule that cannot fail). They stay in as regression guards, but they do not measure what the skill adds.
- 38 passed in at least two more with-skill runs than baseline runs. Most of them test a behaviour the skill teaches:
  - measuring titles in pixels;
  - giving each check a gate;
  - testing bot protection with a forged token;
  - checking schema parity in both directions;
  - reporting rather than failing for what an editor's save produces by construction;
  - adding a control case to each check;
  - recording which assertions ran;
  - stripping `<script>` before prose rules;
  - running worst-case content through the real data file;
  - the day-one host, mobile and motion baseline.
- 5 differ by one run, or tie below 3/3.
- On 3 assertions the baseline did slightly better (2/3 against 3/3). These were the title count, expiry without a deploy, and recommending break-tests. That is within run-to-run noise.

Eval 2 shows the largest gap, and the gap needs reading with care. Baseline plans were competent, but they spent their length on things the assertions do not test: CMS sign-in on Cloudflare, the enquiry form's failure path, and account ownership. They left out the skill's day-one checklist. So eval 2 partly measures whether the agent covers that checklist, not only how well it judges.

## Method

- **Isolation.** Each run had its own folder, outside the repository. The folder held only the fixtures its prompt needed, under neutral paths (`audit-report.md`, `site/`). Agents never saw `evals.json`. Prompt paths were rewritten to match, and the wording was otherwise unchanged.
- **With skill.** The agent read a copy of `SKILL.md`, `references/` and `scripts/` from this repository and followed it.
- **Baseline.** The agent was told not to use any skill. It had the same tools, the same model and the same fixtures.
- **Grading.** One grader per eval saw all six answers, shuffled and labelled A to F. It graded each assertion pass or fail with a short piece of evidence, then checked each assertion across all six answers for consistency. The key mapping labels to conditions was kept outside the graders' folders.

## Limitations

- **Same model throughout.** Runs and graders were the same model. Only one grading pass was made per eval.
- **Blinding was partial.** With-skill answers sometimes name the skill's scripts (`title-width.mjs`, `break-test.mjs`), so a grader could guess the condition.
- **One assertion was changed after grading.** Eval 1's lazy-loading assertion first said hidden lazy images "may never load". No answer said that. Three with-skill answers said the images load late, only when shown, which is closer to how browsers behave. The assertion was reworded to test the behaviour (spotting the lazy and `display:none` interaction), and those three answers were regraded to pass. Under the original wording, eval 1 with the skill scores 25, 24, 24.
- **Environment leakage.** Runs came from a machine whose session context named an Indian company. Several answers assumed an Indian audience and timezone, or quoted that organisation's writing rules. The grading notes tell graders to ignore assumed locale and timezone. The raw answers are not committed for this reason.
- **Small sample.** Three runs per condition is a small sample. Eval 3's with-skill runs ranged from 14 to 18 out of 18.
